What beginners learn first

Beginner classes teach conversational French in plain steps. You hear it, say it, then use it. By the end of level A1 you can introduce yourself, ask simple questions and manage everyday situations in French.

  • Greetings and polite phrases
  • The alphabet and French sounds
  • Introducing yourself and your family
  • Numbers, days, months and time
  • Shopping, food and prices
  • Asking for directions and getting around
  • Daily routine and the present tense
  • Key verbs: être and avoir

Why learn French in Uganda

Uganda borders the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Rwanda and Burundi are close neighbours. French is widely used in all three. It is also an official language in more than 25 countries.

Learners in Uganda choose French for work in tourism, NGOs, trade and international organisations, for studying abroad, and for travel across Africa and Europe.

Basic French phrases for beginners

Start with these eleven phrases. You will use them in your first classes.

  • SalutHi (informal)

  • Merci beaucoupThank you very much
  • S’il vous plaîtPlease
  • Je ne comprends pasI don’t understand

Is French hard for English speakers?

French is one of the easier languages for English speakers because many words look alike, such as nation, table and restaurant. The hardest parts are pronunciation and noun gender (le or la). That is why speaking practice starts in the first lesson.
